================================================================================
1. OBJECTIVE STATEMENT
================================================================================

Implement comprehensive prescription system enabling doctors to prescribe 
medications, manage medication list, check drug interactions and allergies, 
and enable pharmacy fulfillment. System must support multiple dosage forms 
(tablets, capsules, liquids, injectables), variable dosing schedules, 
refill management, and integration with external pharmacy systems. Provide 
clinical decision support for medication recommendations and interaction 
checking. Ensure HIPAA compliance and maintain complete audit trail of all 
prescription modifications.

Expected Outcome:
- Complete prescription workflow from creation → pharmacy submission → patient fulfillment
- Medication interaction and allergy checking before prescription
- Drug database with dosage forms, interactions, contraindications
- Pharmacy integration for prescription transmission
- Patient access to active medications and refill status
- Prescription refill management and renewal tracking

================================================================================
2. BUSINESS LOGIC EXPLANATION
================================================================================

PRESCRIPTION LIFECYCLE:

1. Prescription Initiation
   - Doctor creates prescription during consultation or standalone
   - Medication name selected from drug database (with autocomplete)
   - Dose form selected (tablet 500mg, capsule 250mg, liquid 5mg/5mL, injection)
   - Dose and frequency entered (500mg PO BID, 10mL PO TID, 1 unit IV Q6H)
   - Duration specified (7 days, 2 weeks, 1 month, chronic/ongoing)
   - Quantity calculated (e.g., 500mg tablets × 2/day × 7 days = 14 tablets)
   - Special instructions entered (take with food, do not crush, etc.)
   - Refills specified (0, 3, 6, 11, PRN - as needed)

2. Drug Interaction Checking
   - System retrieves patient's active medications
   - Cross-checks new prescription against all current medications
   - Drug-drug interaction database consulted for:
     ├─ Major interactions (contraindicated together)
     ├─ Moderate interactions (can use with caution/monitoring)
     ├─ Minor interactions (unlikely to cause problems)
     └─ Drug-food interactions (grapefruit juice effects)
   - Alert escalation: Red (stop), Yellow (caution), Green (okay)

3. Allergy Checking
   - System retrieves patient's documented allergies
   - New prescription checked against drug allergens
   - Allergy type checked (true allergy vs intolerance):
     ├─ True IgE allergies → red alert, prescription blocked
     ├─ Intolerance/GI upset → yellow alert, can proceed with caution
     └─ No known allergy → green, proceed
   - Cross-reactivity checking (penicillin allergy → cephalosporin caution)

4. Dosing Verification
   - Dose checked against standard dosing references
   - Age-appropriate dosing for pediatric patients
   - Renal function-adjusted dosing (if kidney disease)
   - Hepatic function-adjusted dosing (if liver disease)
   - Pregnancy category checked (if female of childbearing age)
   - Geriatric dosing considerations (if age > 65)
   - Alerts for unusual doses (too high, too low, etc.)

5. Prescription Attestation
   - Doctor reviews all medication information:
     ├─ Drug name, dose, route, frequency
     ├─ Interaction checks completed
     ├─ Allergy checks completed
     ├─ Dosing appropriateness verified
     └─ Special instructions and refills confirmed
   - Any interaction/allergy alerts must be acknowledged
   - Override documented with reason if alerts overridden
   - e-Signature applied (electronic authentication)

6. Prescription Transmission
   - Prescription sent to patient's preferred pharmacy
   - Electronic transmission (e-prescribing) when possible
   - Fax to pharmacy if e-prescribing unavailable
   - Patient notification (email/SMS) of prescription ready for pickup
   - Pharmacy notification to dispense medication
   - Prescription status tracked (transmitted, received, dispensed, completed)

7. Medication Activation
   - Once dispensed, medication added to patient's active medication list
   - Drug interactions updated with new medication
   - Allergy alerts updated for other providers
   - Care team notified of new medication (alert if relevant)
   - Integration with clinical decision support for future prescriptions

8. Refill Management
   - Patient can request refill (online, phone, pharmacy)
   - Refill availability checked:
     ├─ Refills remaining > 0
     ├─ Refill not too soon (min 75% of days supply used)
     ├─ Medication still appropriate (check for recalls, contraindications)
     └─ Patient insurance coverage verified
   - Doctor approval automated or manual (based on setting)
   - Refill transmitted to pharmacy
   - Medication list updated with new refill date

9. Prescription Expiration & Discontinuation
   - Prescription expires after 1 year (standard)
   - Controlled substances expire sooner (60-90 days)
   - Expired prescription cannot be refilled or dispensed
   - Doctor notified if patient requests refill of expired prescription
   - Patient transitioned to new prescription via renewal workflow
   - Discontinued medications tracked in history for future reference

PRESCRIPTION TYPES:

Acute Medications (7-14 days):
  - Antibiotics (infection treatment)
  - Antivirals (viral infection treatment)
  - Pain medications (acute injury)
  - Anti-nausea medications (temporary)
  - Examples: Amoxicillin 500mg × 7 days, Ibuprofen 400mg × 5 days

Chronic Medications (ongoing):
  - Hypertension (antihypertensives)
  - Diabetes (antidiabetic agents)
  - Asthma (controllers, rescue inhalers)
  - Thyroid disease (levothyroxine)
  - Mental health (antidepressants, anxiolytics)
  - Examples: Lisinopril 10mg daily, Metformin 1000mg BID

As-Needed (PRN) Medications:
  - Pain relief (acetaminophen, ibuprofen, opioids)
  - Allergies (antihistamines)
  - Asthma rescue (albuterol inhaler)
  - Anxiety (short-acting benzodiazepines)
  - Examples: Albuterol inhaler 2 puffs Q4-6H PRN, Ibuprofen 400mg Q6H PRN

Controlled Substances (Special Handling):
  - Opioid pain medications (morphine, oxycodone, hydrocodone)
  - Stimulants (amphetamine, methylphenidate for ADHD)
  - Benzodiazepines (alprazolam, lorazepam, diazepam for anxiety/seizures)
  - Special tracking: DEA license verification, dose limits, shorter prescriptions
  - Requirement: Regular monitoring, pill counts, urine drug screens

Injectable Medications:
  - Insulin for diabetes (subcutaneous)
  - Biologics for rheumatoid arthritis (subcutaneous/IV)
  - Vaccines (intramuscular)
  - Epinephrine for anaphylaxis (intramuscular auto-injector)
  - Require special administration instructions

  patient_id (UUID, FK → patients)
  doctor_id (UUID, FK → doctors)
  clinic_id (UUID, FK → clinics)
  consultation_id (UUID, FK → consultations, nullable)
  medication_id (UUID, FK → medications)
  dose_strength (VARCHAR) - e.g., "500mg", "10mg/mL"
  dose_form (VARCHAR) - e.g., "tablet", "capsule", "liquid", "injection"
  quantity (INT) - number of units to dispense
  route (ENUM: PO, IV, IM, SC, TOP, INH, RECT, OPHTHALMIC, OTIC)
  frequency (VARCHAR) - e.g., "twice daily", "every 6 hours", "as needed"
  frequency_code (VARCHAR) - e.g., "BID", "Q6H", "PRN" (machine-readable)
  duration_value (INT)
  duration_unit (ENUM: DAYS, WEEKS, MONTHS, YEARS)
  special_instructions (TEXT) - "take with food", "do not crush"
  refills_allowed (INT) - 0, 3, 6, 11 (ARB standard), or -1 (unlimited)
  refills_remaining (INT) - tracks refills used
  indication (VARCHAR) - reason for prescription (e.g., "bacterial infection")
  therapeutic_class (VARCHAR) - drug class (e.g., "antibiotic", "NSAID")
  is_controlled_substance (BOOLEAN)
  dea_schedule (ENUM: CI, CII, CIII, CIV, CV, null) - DEA scheduling
  created_at (TIMESTAMP)
  issued_at (TIMESTAMP)

  transmitted_at (TIMESTAMP, nullable)
  pharmacy_id (UUID, FK → pharmacies, nullable)
  pharmacy_npi (VARCHAR, nullable) - pharmacy NPI for transmission
  dispensed_at (TIMESTAMP, nullable)
  dispensed_by_pharmacy (VARCHAR, nullable)
  status (ENUM: DRAFT, ISSUED, TRANSMITTED, RECEIVED, DISPENSED, COMPLETED, EXPIRED, CANCELLED, REFILLED)

  discontinued_at (TIMESTAMP, nullable)
  discontinuation_reason (VARCHAR, nullable)

  is_allergy_override (BOOLEAN)

5. WORKFLOW DESCRIPTION - STEP-BY-STEP PROCESS FLOW
================================================================================

ACUTE PRESCRIPTION WORKFLOW (7-14 days):

Step 1: Prescription Initiation
  └─ Doctor creates prescription during consultation
  └─ Patient and consultation context automatically populated
  └─ Medication selected from database (with autocomplete by generic/brand name)
  └─ Dose, form, strength, route selected
  └─ Frequency entered (BID, TID, Q6H, PRN, etc.)
  └─ Duration specified (7 days for infection, 2 weeks for pain, etc.)
  └─ Quantity auto-calculated (dose × frequency × duration)
  └─ Special instructions entered (take with food, do not crush, etc.)
  └─ Refills determined (0 for acute, 0-3 for short-term)

Step 2: Drug Interaction & Allergy Screening
  └─ System automatically retrieves patient's active medications
  └─ Each medication cross-checked against new prescription:
     ├─ Major interactions detected → RED alert (prescriber warning)
     ├─ Moderate interactions detected → YELLOW alert (caution note)
     ├─ Minor interactions detected → GREEN (informational)
     └─ No interactions found → GREEN (proceed)
  └─ Patient allergies checked against medication:
     ├─ True IgE allergy to medication → RED alert (BLOCK)
     ├─ True allergy to related drug class → YELLOW alert (caution)
     └─ No allergy → GREEN (proceed)
  └─ Dosing checked against standards:
     ├─ Normal dose → GREEN
     ├─ Below normal → YELLOW (may be subtherapeutic)
     ├─ Above normal → YELLOW (verify intended high dose)
     └─ Pediatric/geriatric adjustment applied if needed

Step 3: Prescriber Review & Override Decision
  └─ Doctor reviews all alerts and interaction data
  └─ For RED alerts:
     ├─ Block prescription (cannot be overridden for critical allergies)
     ├─ Or document clinical justification and override with reason
     └─ All overrides logged in audit trail
  └─ For YELLOW alerts:
     ├─ Can proceed with acknowledgment
     ├─ Monitoring recommendations displayed
     └─ Patient counseling points generated
  └─ Doctor confirms:
     ├─ Drug name, dose, route, frequency, duration correct
     ├─ All alerts reviewed and acknowledged
     ├─ Special instructions appropriate
     ├─ Patient education understood
     └─ Ready to issue prescription

Step 4: Prescription Issuance & E-Signature
  └─ Doctor applies electronic signature:
     ├─ Biometric (fingerprint/face recognition)
     ├─ PIN code (4-6 digit)
     ├─ Password (complex password authentication)
     └─ Timestamp recorded with signature
  └─ Prescription status changed to ISSUED
  └─ Prescription locked from further editing
  └─ Audit trail records issuance

Step 5: Pharmacy Transmission
  └─ Prescription automatically transmitted to patient's preferred pharmacy:
     ├─ Electronic (NCPDP e-prescribing standard if pharmacy supports)
     ├─ Fax (if e-prescribing unavailable)
     └─ Status updated to TRANSMITTED
  └─ Pharmacy receives prescription in their system
  └─ Status tracked (received, ready for filling, dispensed)

Step 6: Patient Notification
  └─ Patient notified via email/SMS:
     ├─ Prescription sent to pharmacy
     ├─ Patient can pick up at pharmacy
     ├─ Estimated wait time (usually 24-48 hours)
     └─ Link to view prescription details online
  └─ Patient can request different pharmacy if needed
  └─ Patient can request delay in filling (hold prescription)

Step 7: Pharmacy Fulfillment
  └─ Pharmacy fills prescription:
     ├─ Medication verified against prescription
     ├─ Quantity counted or dispensed
     ├─ Label generated with instructions
     ├─ Drug interaction/allergy check performed by pharmacist
     └─ Prescription marked as DISPENSED in system
  └─ Patient picks up medication:
     ├─ Pharmacy scans prescription
     ├─ Patient identification verified
     ├─ Pickup date recorded
     ├─ Confirmation sent to clinic EMR (prescription COMPLETED)
     └─ Patient receives receipt and medication

Step 8: Medication Activation
  └─ Once dispensed, medication activated in patient's record:
     ├─ Added to active medication list
     ├─ Drug interaction database updated for future prescriptions
     ├─ Care team can see active medication
     └─ Allergy alerts updated for other providers
  └─ Prescription lifecycle complete

CHRONIC PRESCRIPTION WORKFLOW (ongoing):

Steps 1-7: [Same as acute workflow]

Step 8: Automatic Refill & Renewal
  └─ Prescription set for automatic refill:
     ├─ Patient notified prescription is chronic (ongoing)
     ├─ Refill scheduled every 25-28 days (before supply runs out)
     ├─ Patient can opt-in or opt-out of automatic refills
     └─ Pharmacist required to verify patient still wants medication
  └─ Doctor monitors chronic medication effectiveness:
     ├─ Periodic follow-up appointments (every 3-6 months)
     ├─ Labs ordered to monitor (e.g., potassium for ACE inhibitors)
     ├─ Side effects and efficacy reassessed
     └─ Prescription renewed or discontinued based on response

PRN (AS-NEEDED) PRESCRIPTION WORKFLOW:

Steps 1-7: [Same as acute workflow, with PRN frequency]

Differences:
  └─ No automatic refill schedule
  └─ Patient takes medication "as needed" for symptom relief
  └─ Can refill multiple times per year
  └─ Doctor may limit quantity per refill (e.g., "no more than 15 tablets per month")
  └─ Regular monitoring for abuse (especially opioids, benzodiazepines)

REFILL REQUEST WORKFLOW:

Step 1: Patient/Pharmacy Initiates Refill Request
  └─ Patient requests via patient portal:
     ├─ Patient logs in and selects medication to refill
     ├─ Selects quantity needed (usually default quantity)
     ├─ Confirms refill before sending
     └─ Submitted to doctor queue
  └─ Or pharmacy initiates via automation:
     ├─ Pharmacy automation detects medication low in stock
     ├─ Sends refill request to doctor electronically
     ├─ Includes current refill count and days remaining
     └─ Awaits doctor approval

Step 2: Doctor Reviews Refill Request
  └─ Doctor receives refill notification:
     ├─ Patient name, medication, dose, refills remaining
     ├─ Date last filled and days supply
     ├─ Patient compliance (filled on time, early, late)
     ├─ Prior approval history
     └─ Any alerts (age of prescription, recent discontinuation)
  └─ Doctor checks if medication still appropriate:
     ├─ Patient still needs medication (not already switched)
     ├─ Dose still appropriate (patient status unchanged)
     ├─ Refill timing appropriate (not too soon, indicates abuse)
     ├─ Monitoring labs current if required (INR, potassium)
     └─ No new allergies or drug interactions documented

Step 3: Doctor Approval/Denial
  └─ Approval scenario:
     ├─ Doctor approves refill
     ├─ New prescription created as renewal
     ├─ Prescription automatically transmitted to pharmacy
     ├─ Patient notified ("Your prescription has been renewed and sent to pharmacy")
     └─ Process continues with pharmacy fulfillment (Step 5+)
  └─ Denial scenario:
     ├─ Doctor denies refill with reason:
     │  ├─ "Patient needs follow-up appointment first"
     │  ├─ "Labs required before continuation"
     │  ├─ "Medication discontinued"
     │  └─ "Requires prior authorization from insurance"
     ├─ Patient/pharmacy notified with denial reason
     ├─ Patient can request appeal or schedule follow-up appointment
     └─ Doctor schedules follow-up consultation if appropriate
  └─ Conditional approval scenario:
     ├─ Doctor approves limited quantity (e.g., 1-month supply instead of 3-month)
     ├─ Doctor approves with monitoring requirement (e.g., "Check BP before next refill")
     ├─ Doctor approves and schedules follow-up appointment
     └─ Notes added to patient record

12. RISKS & MITIGATION
================================================================================

RISK 1: Drug Interaction Database Accuracy
  │ Impact: Missed interactions could result in patient harm
  │ Probability: LOW
  │ Mitigation:
  │  ├─ Use trusted source (FDA, clinical databases)
  │  ├─ Weekly updates and verification
  │  ├─ Peer review of new interactions
  │  └─ Flag new interactions for manual review

RISK 2: Pharmacy E-Prescribing Integration Complexity
  │ Impact: If integration fails, fallback to fax (delays)
  │ Probability: MEDIUM
  │ Mitigation:
  │  ├─ Work with pharmacy IT early in project
  │  ├─ Use standard NCPDP formats
  │  ├─ Test with pilot pharmacies first
  │  ├─ Implement fax fallback
  │  └─ Clear error messaging and retry logic

RISK 3: Controlled Substance DEA Compliance
  │ Impact: Non-compliance could result in legal/regulatory issues
  │ Probability: LOW
  │ Mitigation:
  │  ├─ Consult with legal/compliance early
  │  ├─ Implement strict DEA requirements
  │  ├─ Regular audits of controlled prescriptions
  │  ├─ Training for prescribers on DEA rules
  │  └─ Periodic external compliance audit

RISK 4: Allergy Override Safety
  │ Impact: Patient safety risk if allergy override not carefully managed
  │ Probability: MEDIUM
  │ Mitigation:
  │  ├─ Block override for true IgE allergies (anaphylaxis risk)
  │  ├─ Require clinical justification for overrides
  │  ├─ Alert pharmacy when allergy override used
  │  ├─ Enhanced pharmacist review for overrides
  │  └─ Patient notification of allergy override
  │  └─ Audit trail of all overrides

RISK 5: Refill Abuse Detection
  │ Impact: Missed opioid/benzodiazepine abuse patterns
  │ Probability: MEDIUM
  │ Mitigation:
  │  ├─ Implement refill timing checks (not too soon)
  │  ├─ Alert for refill seeking at multiple pharmacies
  │  ├─ Integration with state prescription drug monitoring program (PDMP)
  │  ├─ Regular pattern analysis for abuse
  │  └─ Doctor alerts for suspicious refill patterns
